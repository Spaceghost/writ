Runtime path under test (Bend 2.0.39 emitted C): pool_mmap() calls mmap(PROT_READ|PROT_WRITE,
MAP_PRIVATE|MAP_ANON|MAP_NORESERVE) and, on MAP_FAILED, err_fail("reservation failed"), which
prints "bend: reservation failed" to stderr and calls _exit(1). Two reservations reach it at
startup: the 8 GiB CPU corpus (corpus_map tries hinted addresses, then falls back to pool_mmap)
and each lane's 2 GiB evaluation stack plus guard and alternate signal stack (pool_stack).
MAP_NORESERVE does not exempt a mapping from RLIMIT_AS, so ulimit -v makes both fail.

## Result
With 64 MiB, 8 GiB, and 10 GiB of address space the runtime stops before the Bend program runs:
it prints its own "bend: reservation failed", writes nothing to stdout ("hi" is never printed),
and exits 1.
At 8 GiB err_fail is reached with no corpus mapping present, so the 8 GiB corpus reservation is
the one that failed; at 10 GiB the corpus at 0x200000000 is mapped and the failing reservation is
the 2 GiB evaluation stack (pool_stack is the only other caller of pool_mmap). With 11 GiB, and
with no limit, Hello prints "hi" and exits 0, so the failures above come from the limit, not from
the binary. This is CPU execution on one Linux
host; a host that overcommits or ignores the limit is not covered by this record.
