Evaluation

GB-006 lets the shim translate native facts and perform admitted effects. It forbids policy, grants, label decisions, accounting, replay rules, and alternate release decisions. Reading the whole file against that list: the memory half (pages 2 to 6) decides only populate-or-refuse, from an offset and its own tables. The console and time half (page 7) forwards bytes and reads a clock. The channel half (pages 8 and 9) moves records by copy. It never parses a request or a reply, and it writes only one result of its own. Every exit and refusal is fixed at compile time and the same for every caller. Six places could still be argued; my reading of each:

  1. Channel admission and identity binding (writ_mem.cc:1539-1553, writ_mem.cc:1574-1579). This is the strongest candidate: C++ decides which policy forms bind identity, and denies the rest. For the shim: one fixed rule, an integrator-written subject, no grant, and every request still decided by Bend. GB-007 needs a route identity the client cannot choose, and excluding the suffix is what delivers that. Against: who may hold a channel, and as which subject, is an authority fact set outside Bend. I come down on admitted binding, but would ask that the GB-006 rationale name this rule explicitly.
  2. Transport refusal record (writ_mem.cc:1751-1756, writ_mem.cc:1674-1686). This is a result written without Bend. But it is fixed and keyed on the length word alone, its words lie outside the monitor's decision space, and GB-006's own scenario requires it. Not an alternate release decision.
  3. Close event (writ_mem.cc:1610-1642, writ_mem.cc:1727-1735). The shim turns a session close into subject 0 and drops a queued request (counted stale). It translates a native fact; Bend does the unbinding.
  4. Quota margins and RM-session upgrades (writ_mem.cc:575-594, writ_mem.cc:620, writ_mem.cc:648-652). This is "accounting" only in Genode's sense: fixed margins and at most 4 fixed-size upgrades per piece, paid from the component's own quota, with no Writ budget consulted. A host resource rule. Note the PENDING-parent blocking exposure (171-173).
  5. Rules that end the component: the retry cap (writ_mem.cc:510-519, writ_mem.cc:741-742), the timer fail-stops (writ_mem.cc:1272-1286), and the base-hw populated-chunk refusal (writ_mem.cc:849-850). All are bounded mechanical limits that read no Writ fact. They affect availability, not authority, and the retry cap has documented false positives and a livelock case.
  6. Smaller items. Exit 139 (writ_mem.cc:300, writ_mem.cc:794) is a reporting convention with a GB-003 evidence weakness. The hint fallback (writ_mem.cc:985-991) follows Linux semantics, and the runtime decides whether to keep the result. The 1 TiB and 32-slot bounds (writ_mem.cc:930-935) limit growth and lane count. Read-write mprotect on a guard-free range returns 0 without acting (writ_mem.cc:1105-1110).

None of these consults a label, grant, budget, epoch, or replay state, and none can release a result other than Bend's or the fixed transport refusal. The limits that remain are availability and evidence limits already recorded in the inventory. They are not policy, but they should stay listed as limits rather than be read as qualification.
